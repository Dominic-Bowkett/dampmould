Awaab's Law and the wider focus on housing conditions have turned damp and mould
expertise into a **genuinely sought-after skill**. If you're considering it as a
career — or a specialism to add to existing work — this guide sets out a
practical route in.

> **General information.** Routes into surveying vary; check specific
> requirements with professional bodies and employers. See also
> [HHSRS training and qualifications](/hhsrs/training).

## Why now is a good time

Several forces have increased demand for competent damp and mould surveyors:

- **[Awaab's Law](/awaabs-law)** — landlords must investigate and fix damp and
  mould properly and on time, which means they need people who can diagnose it.
- **Heightened scrutiny** of both social and private rented housing.
- **A shortage of genuine independence** — much of the market is treatment-led
  sales rather than impartial diagnosis, leaving room for credible experts.

## The skills that matter

Whatever your starting point, these are the core competencies:

- **Building pathology and damp diagnosis** — understanding condensation,
  penetrating and rising damp, and building performance (heating, insulation,
  ventilation).
- **[HHSRS](/hhsrs) competence** — assessing hazards and risk to a vulnerable
  occupant, and understanding enforcement.
- **Evidence and measurement** — using moisture and environmental readings to
  support conclusions.
- **Clear report writing** — findings that landlords can act on and, if needed,
  rely on.
- **People skills** — handling landlords and anxious tenants fairly and calmly.

## A practical route in

There's **no single mandated path**, but a sensible progression is:

1. **Build a knowledge base** — through a building, construction, surveying or
   housing background, or focused study.
2. **Get specific training** in damp diagnosis and HHSRS. See
   [HHSRS training](/hhsrs/training) and [our courses](/training-courses).
3. **Gain hands-on experience** — ideally alongside an experienced surveyor,
   inspecting real properties.
4. **Pursue relevant qualifications and professional membership** to build
   credibility. <!-- TODO: Dom to name recognised qualifications/bodies. -->
5. **Develop your reporting and independence** — your reputation rests on
   impartial, well-evidenced advice.

## What the work involves

Day to day, a damp and mould surveyor inspects properties, diagnoses the cause of
problems, assesses risk, and reports on the works needed — often where there's a
dispute or a compliance obligation in play. See
[what a damp and mould surveyor does](/damp-mould-surveyor) for the detail.

## Start with training

Structured training from a practising surveyor is the fastest way to build real,
credible competence — particularly in HHSRS and damp diagnosis.
